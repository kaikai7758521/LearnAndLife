# Power Circuit Basics: From LDO to DC-DC
> In one sentence: **power supply design focuses on three things: voltage pairing, small ripple, and controllable heat generation**; first look at voltage drop, current, noise and efficiency when selecting a power supply, and then talk about peripheral components and PCB layout.
> Positioning: Go up one level from the "3.3V power supply" of the smallest system, and explain clearly LDO, Buck/Boost, decoupling, protection and layout. The MCU minimum system itself is `01-最小系统.md`.

## 1. First clarify the constraints of the power supply
For any power supply, answer these questions first:

| Constraints | Typical problems |
|---|---|
| Input range | 12V? 5V USB? Single cell lithium battery 3.0~4.2V? |
| output voltage/accuracy | 3.3V ±2%? 1.8V core voltage? |
| Max/Peak Current | 500mA continuous, 2A peak? |
| ripple/noise | digital load 50mV is enough, ADC/RF should be <10mV? |
| Efficiency/heat generation | Battery powered, sealed enclosure? |
| Transient Response | When the load jumps from 10mA to 1A, how much does the voltage drop? |
| Size/Cost/EMI | Small size, low cost, certified? |

```
输入 → 保护（保险丝/TVS/防反接）→ 转换（LDO/DC-DC）→ 滤波/去耦 → 负载
                                      ↑
                                 使能/时序/反馈
```

## 2. LDO: the simplest and easiest to overheat
### 1. Principle
LDO (Low Dropout Regulator) is essentially a **adjustable resistor**: the excess voltage is dropped through the adjustment tube and a stable voltage is output.
```
Vout ≈ Vref × (1 + R1/R2)
```
### 2. Key parameters
| Parameter | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Dropout | The minimum voltage difference required to maintain stable voltage | When the voltage difference is not enough, the output will follow the input |
| Quiescent current Iq | own power consumption when no load | Battery equipment should be selected with low Iq |
| PSRR | Power supply rejection ratio | Decreases with increasing frequency |
| Output noise | Self-noise | ADC, RF, audio sensitive |
| Stability | Requirements for output capacitor ESR | The bigger the capacitor, the better. Select according to the manual |
| Transient response | Recovery ability when load jumps | Related to loop bandwidth and output capacitance |

### 3. Heat calculation (the most important formula)
```
Ploss = (Vin - Vout) × Iout + Vin × Iq
效率 ≈ Vout / Vin
```
 example:
- 12V → 3.3V / 0.5A: `(12-3.3)×0.5 = 4.35W`, SOT-23 burns directly, must be DC-DC;
- 5V → 3.3V / 0.3A: `(5-3.3)×0.3 = 0.51W`, SOT-223 + copper acceptable;
- 3.6V → 3.3V / 0.2A: `0.06W`, LDO is very suitable.

### 4. Applicable scenarios
✅ Small voltage difference, small current, sensitive to noise (analog/RF/audio), and cost-sensitive;
❌ Large voltage difference, large current, battery power supply (low efficiency), need to boost voltage.

> LDO can only step down, not boost; when the input is lower than `Vout + Dropout`, the output will lose voltage regulation.

## 3. DC-DC: Using switches to exchange efficiency
### 1. Three basic topologies
| Topology | Relationship (ideal) | Scenario |
|---|---|---|
| Buck Buck | `Vout = D × Vin` | 12V/5V → 3.3V/1.8V |
| Boost Boost | `Vout = Vin / (1-D)` | Lithium battery 3.7V → 5V/12V |
| Buck-Boost | Can be raised or lowered | Battery voltage fluctuates across 3.3V |
| Flyback/isolated | Transformer coupled | Requires isolation, multiple outputs |

`D` is the duty cycle. In practice, the diode/synchronous tube voltage drop, inductor DC resistance, and switching loss must also be considered.

### 2. Synchronous and asynchronous
- **non-synchronous**: Schottky diode for freewheeling, simple, cheap, slightly low efficiency;
- **Synchronous**: Use MOSFET instead of diode, with high efficiency and more complex control;
- prioritizes Buck synchronization in low-voltage and high-current scenarios.

### 3. Key peripherals
| Device | Function | Selection Points |
|---|---|---|
| Inductor L | Energy storage, smooth current | Saturation current > Peak current, small DCR, shielded |
| input capacitor | absorbs switching pulse current | low ESR Ceramic, close to chip |
| Output capacitor | Smooth output voltage | Capacity + ESR, meet ripple/transient |
| Feedback resistor | Set output voltage | Accuracy 1% or higher, close to FB pin |
| soft start/compensation | controls the power-on slope and loop stability | Calculate according to the manual, do not change it at will |

Buck inductor ripple current:
```
ΔIL = (Vin - Vout) × D / (L × fsw)
```
 generally takes `ΔIL` as 20%~40% of the output current. If the ripple is too small, the inductance will be large and the transient will be poor; if the ripple is too large, the capacitance and EMI stress will be large.

Output ripple approximation:
```
Vripple ≈ ΔIL / (8 × fsw × Cout) + ΔIL × ESR
```

### 4. Efficiency and loss
- conduction loss: `I² × Rds(on) × D`;
- switching loss: related to `fsw`, gate charge, and voltage swing;
- Inductor copper loss/magnetic loss, capacitor ESR loss;
- enters PFM/pulse skipping at light load, with high efficiency but larger ripple.

The "peak efficiency 95%" of the > datasheet usually occurs at a specific voltage/current point; both light load and full load depend on the curve.

## 4. How to choose LDO vs DC-DC
| Dimensions | LDO | Buck DC-DC |
|---|---|---|
| Efficiency | `Vout/Vin`, extremely low when the pressure difference is large | 85%~95% |
| Fever | Severe when pressure difference is large | Small |
| Noise/ripple | Low | High, filtering required |
| Peripheral | Less (input/output capacitor) | Inductor, capacitor, feedback, compensation |
| Cost/Area | Low/Small | Higher/Large |
| EMI | No switching noise | Yes, layout and shielding required |
| Boost | ❌ | ✅(Boost) |

Common combination: **Buck first drops to close to the target voltage, and then uses LDO post-stage purification** to take into account efficiency and low noise (such as 12V→5V Buck→3.3V LDO for analog circuits).

## 5. Decoupling and filtering: Don’t let noise enter the chip
### 1. Decoupling capacitor
- **Large capacity (10µF/22µF)**: Inlet energy storage, buffering load mutation;
- **Small capacity (0.1µF/1µF)**: close to the chip power pin, providing a high-frequency return path;
- has one 0.1µF for each power pin. Try to connect **as short and thick as possible and** to the power pin and ground;
- via holes will introduce inductance, so drill more via holes if necessary.

### 2. More capacitors are not better
- with different capacitances connected in parallel may form **parallel resonance** at a certain frequency, and the impedance will increase instead;
- pays attention to the ESL/ESR and self-resonant frequency of the capacitor;
- LDO has a stable range for the ESR of the output capacitor. Blindly increasing the capacitance may oscillate.

### 3. Magnetic beads and filtering
- Ferrite Bead is resistive at high frequencies and is often used for digital/analog power isolation;
- Pay attention to the rated current and DC resistance, and the voltage drop cannot affect the load;
- magnetic beads + capacitor may form LC resonance, so add a damping resistor if necessary;
The - RF/ADC power supply can be supplied with LC filtering or dedicated low-noise LDO.

## 6. Protection, timing and battery
### 1. Input protection
| Protection | device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Overcurrent/short circuit | Fuse, PTC, eFuse | Limit current to prevent fire |
| Overvoltage | TVS, OVP chip | Absorb surge/static |
| Anti-reverse connection | P-MOS/ideal diode | Anti-reverse connection plate for power supply |
| Surge/hot swap | Soft start, hot swap controller | Limit power-on shock |
| Over temperature | OTP, temperature switch | Shut down or derate |

### 2. Power-on sequence
- multi-channel power supply (core voltage, IO, analog) must be powered on in sequence according to chip requirements;
- is connected in series with `EN` and `PG` (Power Good), and the next stage is started after the previous stage is stable;
- Sequence errors may cause latch-up, power leakage, and startup failure;
The - reset signal should be released after the power supply stabilizes.

### 3. Battery and USB
- Single cell lithium battery: 3.0~4.2V, requires overcharge/overdischarge/overcurrent protection;
- charging chip follows CC/CV process, pay attention to charging current and heat dissipation;
- USB 5V power supply must comply with enumeration and current negotiation; USB-C PD requires a protocol chip, **cannot directly draw power beyond the rated current of**;
- battery-powered equipment focuses on light load efficiency and quiescent current.

## 7. PCB layout: half the success or failure of the power supply
### 1. Buck’s “hot loop”
The smaller the ```
输入电容 → 上管 → 下管 → GND → 输入电容
```
 loop area, the better:
The input capacitor of - is close to the chip `VIN` and `GND`;
The traces between - upper tube/down tube/inductor are short and wide;
The - switch node (SW) copper area is moderate: too large to radiate EMI, too small to have poor heat dissipation;
Avoid sensitive signals under the - inductor, and slot or shield if necessary.

### 2. Feedback and sampling
The - FB trace should be kept away from the SW node and inductor to avoid coupling noise;
The - feedback resistor is close to the FB pin, and the sampling point is as close to the load as possible (remote sampling);
- high-precision scenes use Kelvin connection.

### 3. Ground and heat dissipation
- complete ground plane, single point/zone connection between power ground and signal ground;
Drill multiple vias from the heat dissipation pad on the bottom of the - chip to the ground plane;
- estimates the temperature rise based on `θJA` and copper area, don’t just look at the ambient temperature;
- Inductors and MOSFETs are the main heat sources and should be kept away from temperature-sensitive devices.

## 8. Measurement and debugging
### 1. Ripple measurement
- uses **probe ground spring**, do not use long ground clamp (it will pick up switching noise);
- oscilloscope bandwidth limit is 20MHz, AC coupled;
- is measured at both ends of the output capacitor and the load end;
- records three states: full load/light load/transient.

### 2. Common waveform problems
| Phenomenon | Possible causes |
|---|---|
| Output oscillation | Loop instability, output capacitor ESR mismatch, compensation error |
| Power-on overshoot | Insufficient soft start, feedback delay, load mutation |
| The ripple is too large | The inductor is too small, the output capacitance is insufficient, and the layout is poor |
| Light load whistling | PFM frequency falls into audio frequency, inductor/capacitor vibration |
| Severe fever | Low efficiency, conduction loss, insufficient heat dissipation |
| startup failure | Input drop, enable timing, current limit trigger |

### 3. Efficiency and thermal testing
- uses electronic load to sweep the current and record `Vin×Iin` and `Vout×Iout`;
- thermal imager looks at hot spots and thermocouples measure key components;
- is aged at full load for a long time, and the temperature rise and drift are observed.

## 9. Selection process and examples
### 1. Process
1. lists input/output/current/noise/efficiency/size constraints;
2. estimates the LDO heat generation, if it exceeds 0.5~1W, give priority to DC-DC;
3. select topology (Buck/Boost/Buck-Boost/Isolation);
4. chip selection: switching frequency, integrated MOS, quiescent current, protection function;
5. calculates inductance, input/output capacitance, and feedback resistor;
6. design protection and timing;
7. layout, proofing, and measurement of ripple/efficiency/temperature rise/EMI.

### 2. Example
| Required | Recommended |
|---|---|
| 12V → 5V / 2A | Synchronous Buck |
| 5V → 3.3V / 200mA (analog) | Low noise LDO |
| 3.7V lithium battery → 5V / 1A | Boost |
| 12V → 3.3V / 3A | Synchronous Buck |
| 24V → ±12V (Op Amp) | Flyback/Isolation or Buck-Boost + Charge Pump |
| 5V → 1.8V / 1A (FPGA core) | Synchronous Buck + Timing Control |

## 10. Common misunderstandings
1. ❌ LDO is simple, so you can use it casually: heating under a large voltage difference will directly burn the device;
2. ❌ LDO efficiency is estimated at 90%: linear adjustment, the efficiency is about `Vout/Vin`;
3. ❌ DC-DC ripple must be large: reasonable layout and filtering can achieve several mV;
4. ❌ The bigger the inductor, the better: large inductors have poor transient response, large size and high cost;
5. ❌ The larger the output capacitance, the more stable it is: LDO has an ESR stable range, and DC-DC loops must be considered;
6. ❌ Just look at the datasheet. Typical values: Design according to the worst case (highest temperature, lowest Vin, maximum load);
7. ❌ Light load efficiency is not important: the battery device is under light load most of the time;
8. ❌ Connect the power pin casually: if the decoupling capacitor is far away, it means that it is not added;
9. ❌ USB 5V can be chosen at will 2A: no enumeration/no negotiation will trigger protection or burn the wire;
10. ❌ As long as the schematic is correct: half of the power supply performance depends on the PCB layout.

## 11. Summary
- first calculate the pressure difference and heat generation, and then decide whether LDO or DC-DC;
- LDO is superior in simplicity and low noise, DC-DC is superior in efficiency and voltage boost;
- Inductor, capacitor, feedback, protection and timing, none of them can be missing;
The - power supply is a "layout-sensitive" circuit: small thermal loop, good ground decoupling, clean feedback, and more efficient than stacking.
