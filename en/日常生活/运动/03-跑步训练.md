# Running training: from entry to daily routine
> In a word: Running progress depends on **gradual running volume + adequate recovery + basic strength**, not pushing it to the limit every time.
> Note: The following is general exercise science and does not constitute medical advice; those with cardiovascular disease, joint problems, pregnancy or long-term inactivity should consult a doctor first.

## 1. Before starting
- Choose a pair of **running shoes that fit your feet and have suitable cushioning**. They don’t have to be the most expensive;
- The first week is mainly "brisk walking + jogging", do not go directly to 5 kilometers;
- Find a flat, safe and well-lit route, and try to avoid motor vehicle lanes;
- records resting heart rate, weight, and sleep as a baseline for subsequent comparison;
- Avoid midday in summer, warm up fully in winter, and move indoors when the air is bad.

## 2. Four basic trainings
| Type | Strength | Feeling | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy running Easy | Maximum heart rate 60%~70% | Able to speak completely | Lay the foundation, recover, and increase running volume |
| Long-distance jogging LSD | 60%~70% | Able to chat, but a little tired at the end | Endurance, fat utilization |
| Tempo running Tempo | 80%~90% | Can only speak short sentences | Increase lactate threshold |
| Interval | 90%+ | Speechless | Increase maximum oxygen uptake and speed |

In the novice stage, **80% and above should be easy to run**, don't "fight" every day.

How to judge the strength of ### 
- **Conversation test**: Can speak a complete sentence → easy; can only speak a few words → high intensity;
- **RPE Conscious exertion**: 1~10 points, easy run 3~4, rhythm run 6~7, interval 8~9;
- **Heart rate**: `最大心率 ≈ 220 - 年龄` is just a rough estimate, individual differences are large, and it is more reliable to combine it with body feeling.

## 3. 8-week plan for newbies (3 times a week)
| Week | Training A | Training B | Training C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walk briskly for 30 minutes | Walk 3 minutes + jog 1 minute × 6 | Run easily for 15 minutes |
| 2 | 35 minutes of brisk walking | 2 minutes of walking + 2 minutes of jogging × 6 | 18 minutes of easy running |
| 3 | 20 minutes of easy running | 1 minute of walking + 3 minutes of jogging × 6 | 20 minutes of easy running |
| 4 | Easy running for 25 minutes | Jogging for 25 minutes (can walk) | Easy running for 20 minutes |
| 5 | Easy jogging for 30 minutes | Jogging for 30 minutes | Easy running for 25 minutes |
| 6 | Easy run for 30 minutes | Tempo run for 15 minutes (you can walk in between) | Easy run for 30 minutes |
| 7 | Easy jogging for 35 minutes | Jogging for 35 minutes | Easy running for 25 minutes |
| 8 | Easy running for 30 minutes | Continuous jogging 5 kilometers (no speed limit) | Walking/stretching |

Principles:
- The total weekly running volume increase is controlled at around **10%** (just experience value, not a hard indicator);
- Leave at least one day between runs to allow your body to recover;
- If it hurts, reduce the dosage and don’t “run through the injury”;
- The plan can be adjusted according to the pace of life, and persistence is more important than perfection.

## 4. Running posture and breathing
- The body leans forward slightly, leaning forward from the ankles rather than the waist;
- Relax your shoulders and swing your arms back and forth, not sideways;
- Don’t take too long a stride to avoid your knees from locking when your heels strike;
- **Step frequency 170~180 steps/minute is not a hard standard**. Small steps and fast frequency are usually more labor-saving, but individual differences are large;
- Breathe naturally. You can inhale through the nose, exhale through the mouth or both at the same time. The key is to have a stable rhythm;
- When going uphill, shorten your stride length and maintain your cadence; when going downhill, control your speed and don't rush.

## 5. Strength and flexibility
Just 20 minutes twice a week:

| Action | Number of groups | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Squat/Box Squat | 3×10 | Hip and leg strength |
| Glute Bridge / Single Leg Glute Bridge | 3×12 | Glute muscle and hip stability |
| Heel raise | 3×15 | Calf, Achilles tendon |
| Single-leg deadlift | 3×8/side | Balance, posterior chain |
| Side plank | 3×30 seconds/side | Core, outer hip |
| clam opening and closing | 3×15/side | gluteus medius |

- Do **dynamic warm-up** before running (high leg raise, back kick, walking lunge);
- Do static stretching after running, focusing on the calves, the back of the thighs, and the front of the hips;
- Try not to schedule strength training and long-distance running on the same day.

## 6. Common injuries and prevention
| Problems | Common Causes | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Medial tibia pain (tibial stress syndrome) | Running volume increased too fast, hard ground, old shoes | Reduce weight, apply ice, change shoes, strengthen calves |
| Front knee pain (runner's knee) | Weak gluteal/quadriceps muscles, excessive stride length | Strength training, shortening stride length, reducing weight |
| Plantar fasciitis | Running volume, insufficient shoe support, tight calves | Relax the soles/calves, change shoes, reduce weight |
| Iliotibial band syndrome (pain on the outside of the knee) | Weak gluteus medius, frequent downhills | Gluteus medius training, avoid downhills, stretching |
| Achilles tendinitis | Sudden acceleration/increase, tight calves | Reduce weight, eccentric heel raises, seek medical attention if necessary |

Red Flags (See Medical Immediately):
- Chest pain, syncope, abnormal heart palpitations;
- Pain continues to worsen, pain occurs at night, and swelling is obvious;
- Suspected stress fracture (localized sharp pain, pressing pain);
- Unable to walk with weight bearing after injury.

## 7. Nutrition, hydration and sleep
- Daily guarantee **carbohydrates + protein**, do not run long distances on an empty stomach;
- Eat digestible carbohydrates (bananas, bread) 1 to 2 hours before running;
- Supplement protein + carbohydrate within 1 to 2 hours after running to help recovery;
- For short-term (<60 minutes) ordinary jogging, plain water is usually enough; for long distances/profuse sweating, consider electrolytes;
- Do not drink too much water (risk of hyponatremia), drink according to thirst and sweat;
- sleeps for 7~9 hours. When sleep is insufficient, priority is given to reducing training volume.

## 8. Recording and advancement
It is recommended to record each time:
- distance, duration, average pace;
- average heart rate, maximum heart rate, RPE;
- sleep, weight, pain location;
- Weather, route, shoes.

Advanced ideas:
1. is first stabilized 3 times a week for 8 weeks;
2. adds another long distance;
3. When you want to speed up, only add a quality class (tempo run or interval) once a week;
4. Start tapering 1 to 3 weeks before the competition (taper, the longer the distance, the longer the tapering);

## 9. 5km/10km race tips
- Reduce running volume and ensure sleep one week before the race;
- Eat a digestible breakfast 2 to 3 hours in advance on the day of competition;
- The first 1 kilometer is 5~10 seconds slower than the target pace to avoid starting too fast;
- fine-tune according to the body feeling in the middle, and then accelerate in the last 1~2 kilometers;
- After the game, take a walk, hydrate, and then stretch.

## 10. Common misunderstandings
1. ❌ No progress without pain: Pain is a signal, not a medal;
2. ❌ Run every day: recovery days are equally important, 3 times a week is enough for novices;
3. ❌ Static stretching before running: Dynamic warm-up before running is more suitable;
4. ❌ The cadence must be 180: it varies from person to person, and blindly changing the cadence may be more tiring;
5. ❌ Expensive shoes must be better: fit, comfort, and suit your foot shape are the most important;
6. ❌ Drink water if you sweat a lot: it may dilute blood sodium, so replenish electrolytes as needed;
7. ❌ The greater the running volume, the better: exceeding recovery capacity is the risk of injury;
8. ❌ Only running without strength training: Weak hip and leg core is the source of many injuries.

## 11. Summary
- Newbies should first establish the **rule**, and then talk about speed;
- Easy running accounts for the majority, and quality classes are held once a week;
- Strength, sleep, and nutrition are invisible “training”;
- If red flag symptoms appear, seek medical attention immediately and do not take it hard.
